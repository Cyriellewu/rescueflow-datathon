## Policy lab (SIMULATION)

Fleet, handover and cross-borough travel are **scenario assumptions**; units per borough calibrated to the pre-test all-call p90 dispatch wait: Bronx 50, Brooklyn 63, Manhattan 52, Queens 48, Richmond / Staten Island 12. Demand ×1.0 (28,386 calls in the evaluation week). Shock: Brooklyn handover ×2.0 for hours 62–70 of the week. Low-acuity calls are held while a borough's idle units are ≤ 10% of its fleet (FDNY practice). Travel: spatial/spatial_data/borough_travel_matrix.json (cross-borough), FDNY medians in-borough; traffic: NYC DOT i4gi-tjb9 hourly speeds, borough median of prior 8 weeks / hour's speed. Arms watch the low-acuity queue; reactive trigger at 10 waiting.

## Limitations

- Historical replay of published records, not a live CAD feed; FDNY publishes with a lag of months.
- The backlog is reconstructed from timestamps after the fact; real-time availability of each field is assumed, not verified.
- FDNY's response measure is not the Comptroller's end-to-end time; they are not compared on one axis.
- Bottleneck tags and at-hospital counts are contributing indicators, not causes; incident close ≠ unit available.
- Policy-lab results depend on scenario assumptions and say nothing about real-world minutes saved.
