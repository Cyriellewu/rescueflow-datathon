# External check of the policy lab's assumptions

Generated by `scripts/validate_turnaround.py`. Do the policy lab's unit-cycle assumptions match real EMS records?

| Assumption | Policy lab | NYC | San Francisco | Indiana | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time at the hospital (median, min) | 13.9 | 41.1 | 42.6 | – | shorter than real |
| Time on scene (median, min) | 13.9 | 21.3 | 16.5 | – | close to real |
| Transport to hospital (median, min) | 12.0 | 12.4 | 15.6 | 13.0 | close to real |
| Share transported | 65% | 63% | 69% | 75% | close to real |

Medians in minutes. Full quantiles, histograms and yearly medians are in `external_validation.json`.

## Detail

**Time at the hospital.** Lab: handover ~ Exponential(mean 20 min) → median 13.9, P90 46.1.
- NYC FDNY (real, proxy), hospital arrival -> incident close: median 41.1, P25–P75 36–46, P90 52, n = 3,595,103; by year {2023: 39.9, 2024: 41.8, 2025: 41.0, 2026: 42.3}
- San Francisco ambulances (real), hospital arrival -> unit available: median 42.6, P25–P75 33–55, P90 68, n = 311,726; by year {2023: 43.5, 2024: 42.4, 2025: 42.1, 2026: 42.6}
- Riverside County, CA (AMR, real aggregate), ambulance ED visits held > 30 min after arrival: 21.4% of 587,958 visits (lab: 22.3%); mean time beyond 30 min among those held 29.9 min; by year {2012: 0.182, 2013: 0.191, 2014: 0.249, 2015: 0.243, 2016: 0.204}

_Reading:_ Until the unit is free again, real ambulances spend a median of 41–43 min at the hospital; the lab's handover draw has a median of 13.9. The handover step itself looks right: in Riverside County 21% of ambulance ED visits were held over 30 min, and the lab gives 22%. What the lab leaves out is the time after handover (cleaning, restocking, paperwork): it frees the unit the moment the handover ends, so it under-states how long hospitals tie up units.

**Time on scene.** Lab: on scene ~ Exponential(mean 20 min) → median 13.9, P90 46.1.
- NYC FDNY (real), first unit on scene -> left for hospital: median 21.3, P25–P75 15–30, P90 40, n = 3,604,589; by year {2023: 20.4, 2024: 20.9, 2025: 21.9, 2026: 22.5}
- San Francisco ambulances (real), unit on scene -> transport start: median 16.5, P25–P75 11–23, P90 30, n = 312,202; by year {2023: 17.3, 2024: 16.4, 2025: 16.4, 2026: 15.9}

_Reading:_ Real crews spend a median of 16–21 min on scene with patients they transport; the lab's mean of 20 min matches, but its exponential draw is wider than the real spread (lab P90 46 min vs real 40).

**Transport to hospital.** Lab: transport ~ lognormal(median 12 min, sigma 0.35) → median 12.0, P90 18.8.
- NYC FDNY (real), left for hospital -> hospital arrival: median 12.4, P25–P75 9–17, P90 22, n = 3,595,582; by year {2023: 11.8, 2024: 12.6, 2025: 12.3, 2026: 12.9}
- San Francisco ambulances (real), transport start -> hospital arrival: median 15.6, P25–P75 10–23, P90 31, n = 311,173; by year {2023: 14.4, 2024: 16.6, 2025: 15.6, 2026: 16.0}
- Indiana statewide EMS (real), scene -> destination (file's PROVIDER_TO_DESTINATION_MINS, undocumented): median 13.0, P25–P75 8–20, P90 32, n = 846,474

_Reading:_ Real drives to the hospital take a median of 12–16 min; the lab's 12 min matches NYC closely (12.4).

## Datasets reviewed

| Place | Dataset | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| New York City | FDNY EMS Incident Dispatch Data (76xm-jjuj) | all four checks; hospital time via incident close (proxy) |
| San Francisco | SFFD Calls for Service (data.sf.gov nuek-vuh3) | all four checks; unit-level hospital -> available |
| Indiana (statewide) | EMS runs 2024, NEMSIS/ImageTrend (hub.mph.in.gov) | transport and share; timestamps are date-only and there is no transfer-of-care or back-in-service time |
| Riverside County, CA | Ambulance Patient Offload Delay Data (8ueb-m2qq), 2012-2016 | handover tail: share of ED visits held over 30 min (aggregates only, ends 2016) |
| Cincinnati, OH | CFD NFIRS incident data (2018-2021) | not used: fire incident reports with unit clear time, no hospital timestamps |
| Connecticut (statewide) | Fire Department Incidents 2012-2022 (qem9-rt8k) | not used: fire incidents, last unit cleared only |
| Seattle | Real Time Fire 911 Calls (kzjm-xkqj) | not used: call time, type and location only, no unit timestamps |

## Caveats

- Other places are references for the size and shape of each interval, not proof about NYC.
- NYC has no unit-available time in public data; incident close stands in for it in the hospital check.
- San Francisco and Indiana are unit-level records; a call with two ambulances counts twice.
- Hospital arrival -> available (SF) includes handover, cleaning, restocking and paperwork; Riverside's offload delay counts only the handover step.
- In the lab, a unit also queues for a handover slot when slots are full, so its simulated hospital time can exceed the handover draw.
- Indiana is statewide (urban and rural); its transport minutes come from an undocumented field and its timestamps are date-only.
- Riverside County's offload data are monthly aggregates by hospital and end in 2016.
- Durations above 240 min (hospital) or 120 min (scene, transport), and zero or negative ones, are excluded as data errors.
